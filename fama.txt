FAMA — FActor Mining Agent
Renmin University of China / Kunming University of Science and Technology
Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: ACL 2024 · August 2024

Problem: Traditional symbolic factor mining is interpretable but searches a very large expression space inefficiently, while neural factor models can extract stronger signals but are much harder to interpret. A naïve LLM approach has another problem: if the prompt contains a small set of similar existing factors, the model tends to generate more factors with the same structure and repeatedly explores unproductive patterns.

Idea: Use an LLM to generate new symbolic factors, but give it better examples and better search history. FAMA adds two mechanisms. Cross-Sample Selection (CSS) clusters existing factors and deliberately chooses low-correlation factors from different clusters as in-context examples, reducing repetitive generation. Chain-of-Experience (CoE) records sequences of increasingly effective factors and feeds these successful search paths back to the LLM as examples of how earlier formulas were improved. The LLM then directly outputs a new symbolic expression, for example moving from expressions such as "close/open" and "(high*low)^0.5" to a new factor such as "((close-open)/(high-low))^0.5".

Novelty: FAMA does not simply ask an LLM to invent another factor. It controls what the LLM sees before generation. CSS tells the model: “look at structurally different factors, not several versions of the same one.” CoE tells it: “look at the sequence of changes that previously led to better factors.” In that sense, FAMA is an early example of making diversity and previous successful search experience part of the LLM prompt itself. The authors describe it as a neural-symbolic approach that uses LLMs to bridge numerical factor evidence and explicit symbolic expressions.

Reported result: The authors test FAMA on S&P 500 stocks using open, close, high, low and volume data. Their main test period is 2021, with only about 10% of the full historical dataset used by FAMA for training/validation. FAMA(CI-7) reports RankIC 0.054 and RankICIR 0.485, compared with 0.048 / 0.379 for FactorVAE, the strongest neural baseline in their table. The paper reports that combining CSS and CoE works better than either mechanism alone and that factor quality generally improves with additional CoE iterations.

In the paper's portfolio simulation, factors are weighted using past RankIC; stocks in the top 20% by factor value are bought and sold the following day. The authors report 38.4% annualised return, 4.9% annualised volatility, and a Sharpe ratio reported in the paper as 667.2% (equivalent to approximately 6.67 if read as a ratio rather than a percentage). These are paper-reported simulation results and should not be treated as an institutional trading benchmark.

Reference: Zhiwei Li, Ran Song, Caihong Sun, Wei Xu, Zhengtao Yu & Ji-Rong Wen, “Can Large Language Models Mine Interpretable Financial Factors More Effectively? A Neural-Symbolic Factor Mining Agent Model,” Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: ACL 2024, pp. 3891–3902. DOI: 10.18653/v1/2024.findings-acl.233.
